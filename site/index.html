the big one

Source 2 engine ships, and half the modding scene breathes again

Valve quietly pushed the Source 2 toolchain to everyone, and the first wave of ports landed inside 48 hours.

For years the community treated Source 2 as a rumour with a beta attached. That changed overnight: the toolchain is now part of the default install, and three high-profile mods published playable builds before lunch.

The interesting part is not the renderer. It is the Hammer editor workflow: map files are now plain assets, so a workshop upload no longer needs a custom launcher.

Modders are already reporting dramatically shorter iteration loops. One mapper timed a full rebuild at 40 seconds, down from nine minutes on the same machine.

Why it matters The modding pipeline is the reason half these games still have players.

Nine minutes to 40 seconds. Somewhere a mapper is crying into a warm energy drink.

ai

Agent frameworks converge on the same boring answer: files and logs

Three vendors, one architecture, zero magic.

The newest generation of agent tooling dropped the orchestration graph and kept the filesystem. State lives in a directory; the model reads it, writes it, and every step is a line in a log.

It is less impressive in a keynote and far more debuggable at 3am, which is the only review that matters here.

Why it matters Boring architectures survive contact with production.
